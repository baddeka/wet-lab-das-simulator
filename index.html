A platform for biomarker detection,
not a sensor for one

One plasmid skeleton that never changes — origin, resistance, reporter, and two fixed insertion sites. Between them sits a module that recognises one biomarker. Another biomarker, another module, same skeleton.

The strongest module in the library is not the one this project started with. Putrescine, read by PuORh, reaches AUC 0.933 at the reader with sensitivity and specificity 86%. Diacetylspermine reaches 0.749 on the same scale, and only after the sample is concentrated.

Colorectal cancer stays as the worked example, not as the project. Every figure on this page is computed from data/sensors.csv, data/clinical.csv, data/platform.csv, data/scenarios.csv, data/out_of_scope.csv and written into the markup at build time.

Architecture

Two module types, because one is not enough

Type A exists for only a handful of analytes, because oxidases are scarce. Type B is what the platform falls back on where no oxidase is published, and having both is what makes this an architecture rather than one device. A sensor that carries its own fluorophore is a third thing, and is left out on purpose: it would replace the skeleton instead of plugging into it.

Why most candidates fail

Where each module’s constant falls against its analyte

A module reads a patient difference best when its Michaelis or dissociation constant sits inside the clinical range. The diamond is that constant, the bar is the published healthy-to-sick span, and the distance between them predicts the verdict better than anything else in the screen.

Every constant plotted here is a Km: all of these are type A modules. No type B module appears at all — the only one in the library with a published Kd is BetI, and choline has no clinical distribution to plot it against. That gap in the data, not the chemistry, is what currently limits the second module type. Sitting far below the constant is survivable — the branch is simply first order there — but it costs enzyme loading and signal. Sitting far above means saturation, which no amount of sample preparation recovers.

The screen

Every module against every study

Two gates, in order. Stage 1 asks whether the published cohorts separate at all — from the reported AUC where one exists, otherwise from Welch’s t and Cohen’s d rebuilt from the summary statistics. A biomarker that fails here is not worth a module however good the protein. Stage 2 asks whether a module could read that difference, and runs only on what survived. Rejections are listed with the stage that produced them: a screen that reports only its successes is not a screen.

Panels

One marker rarely gives usable specificity

Urinary polyamines rise in colorectal cancer — and also in benign gastrointestinal disease, which is exactly what costs the published assays their specificity. Reading several modules at once is the way out, and against a fixed skeleton that is a cloning job rather than a redesign.

How much a panel gains depends on how its markers co-vary, and that needs them measured in the same patients. Not one source in this library reports it. So each panel is given as a range over every correlation from 0 to 0.7, never as a single number, and the verdict is taken from the worst point of that range. Where the upper end comes from a suppressor effect — correlated markers cancelling shared variation — the row says so, because that gain needs the correlation known exactly and does not survive estimating it.

Catalytic efficiency spans 142× across these enzymes; the device fold spans 1.06×. Below Km the branch is first order in kcat/Km, so efficiency buys lower enzyme loading, not signal — the ceiling is set by the cell’s own peroxide flux, which no enzyme moves.

The model underneath

Two compartments, nothing fitted

Cytoplasm and medium are separate compartments exchanging H2O2 at k_mem ≈ 90.0 s⁻¹. The cytoplasm is quasi-steady on a millisecond timescale; the medium is integrated explicitly, because at low cell density it equilibrates over minutes. Anchors: endogenous production P_endo ≈ 14.0 µM/s and resting cytoplasmic 0.02 µM, which together force k_scav ≈ 700 s⁻¹; OxyR half-activation at 0.1 µM. The parameters over-determine the system, and the check is that they reproduce a quantity none of them was set from: the medium-to-cytoplasm peroxide gradient, which comes out at 8.8× against a measured value of about nine.

The competitor is a flux, not a threshold. E. coli makes peroxide continuously, so the question was never whether the biomarker trips OxyR but whether its trickle is audible over the cell’s own. That is why fewer cells and a shorter read both help — and why deleting the scavengers does not: it lifts resting peroxide above the OxyR set point, and the reporter starts switched on with nowhere to go.

The worked example, with every parameter exposed: the diacetylspermine simulator.

Honest limits

What is missing, and what was left out deliberately

No module here has been validated experimentally.

This is a designed and modelled platform, not a working one. The 7 pairs that clear both gates have cleared them on paper: against published kinetics, published cohorts and a model whose only external check is that it reproduces a peroxide gradient it was not fitted to. Nothing here has met a patient sample.

Needed and missing

  • DiAcSpm — DAS oxidase (colorimetric assay) (type A) has no km_uM, kcat_s, so no response can be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Putrescine — PuuR (type B) has no kd_uM, so no response can be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Sarcosine — SouR (PA4184) (type B) has no kd_uM, so no response can be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Lactate — LldR (type B) has no kd_uM, so no response can be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Spermidine — clinical data exists but no module in the library recognises it.

Deliberately out of scope

These are real, published, well-characterised sensors. They are excluded because they carry their own fluorophore: binding changes their fluorescence directly, with no transcription and no reporter gene, so they replace the fixed skeleton instead of plugging into it. A different architecture, not a module.

  • iPASnFR-H — Spermine, Kd 15 µM
  • iPASnFR-L — Spermine, Kd 130 µM
  • iPASnFR-H — Spermidine, Kd 15 µM
  • iPASnFR-L — Spermidine, Kd 2300 µM
  • R-iLACCO1 (purified) — Lactate, Kd 74 µM
  • R-iLACCO1 (in situ) — Lactate, Kd 680 µM
  • eLACCO2.1 (purified) — Lactate, Kd 1900 µM
  • eLACCO2.1 (in situ) — Lactate, Kd 580 µM

The platform covers small molecules recognised by an oxidase or by a metabolite-binding transcription factor; protein markers do not fit it at all. Every module shares one ceiling on sensitivity, set by the host’s own peroxide flux rather than by any module.
