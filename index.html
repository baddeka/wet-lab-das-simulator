A platform for biomarker detection,
not a sensor for one

One plasmid skeleton that never changes - origin, resistance, reporter, and two fixed insertion sites. Between them sits a module that recognises one biomarker. Another biomarker, another module, same skeleton.

The strongest module in the library is not the one this project started with. Putrescine, read by PuORh, reaches AUC 0.936 at the reader with sensitivity and specificity 86%. Diacetylspermine reaches 0.747 on the same scale, and only after the sample is concentrated.

Colorectal cancer stays as the worked example, not as the project. Every figure and every number on this page is computed from data/sensors.csv, data/clinical.csv, data/platform.csv, data/scenarios.csv, data/out_of_scope.csv and written into the markup at build time; the page itself calculates nothing.

1The platform

Two module types, because one is not enough

1.1One skeleton, two insertion sites

Everything outside the two insertion sites is fixed: the origin, the resistance marker and the reporter never change between modules. That is the whole claim of the design - a second biomarker is a cloning job, not a redesign - and it is also the constraint that decides what counts as a module at all.

1.2Why there are two module types

Type A exists for only a handful of analytes, because oxidases are scarce: of 7 biomarkers in this library, 5 have a published oxidase with both constants. Type B is the fallback where no oxidase exists, and having both is what makes this an architecture rather than one device. A sensor that carries its own fluorophore is a third thing and is left out on purpose - binding changes its fluorescence directly, with no transcription and no reporter gene, so it would replace the skeleton instead of plugging into it.

2The model

Two compartments, nothing fitted

2.1What it has to answer

The question is not whether the biomarker can make hydrogen peroxide - it can - but whether that peroxide is audible over the peroxide E. coli makes anyway. So the model is built around a competition between two fluxes, and the quantity it reports is a ratio between two samples rather than an absolute brightness.

Assumptions. (1) The cytoplasm is quasi-steady on a millisecond timescale, so it follows the medium; the medium is integrated explicitly, because at low cell density it equilibrates over minutes. (2) Peroxide crosses the membrane as a single first-order exchange in both directions. (3) Scavenging is one first-order constant, which is adequate in the sub-micromolar range this design works in and is not adequate above it. (4) OxyR activation is instantaneous relative to transcription. (5) The reporter is diluted by growth and not degraded. (6) Both clinical cohorts are log-normal on the concentration scale, which section 3.3 tests rather than assumes.

The competitive sum is exact only at a Hill coefficient of 1, and the platform assumes 1 because no source reports a Hill coefficient for any sensor in the library. Where that matters for a verdict, the candidate's row says so.

2.4The constants, and what fixed each one

The parameters over-determine the system, which is what makes it falsifiable rather than merely plausible. Three measurements in one paper[1] fix the peroxide half of it: a production rate of 14 µM/s, a resting cytoplasmic level of 0.02 µM, which together force kscav ≈ 700 s⁻¹, and a membrane permeability that gives kmem ≈ 90 s⁻¹. OxyR's half-activation point comes from an in-vitro titration[2]. The check is that these reproduce a quantity none of them was set from - the medium-to-cytoplasm gradient, which comes out at 8.8× against a measured value of about nine.

2.5Where OxyR sits before the sample arrives

Resting cytoplasmic peroxide is Pendo / kscav, so deleting the scavengers moves the strain along the activation curve rather than making it more sensitive. At the wild-type rate the reporter sits 17% activated, leaving 83% of its range for the analyte to use. In a catalase and peroxidase triple knockout it starts at 97%, with 3% left (Figure 3).

Read as exponents, it predicts that S/B falls as 1 / φ, as 1 / Pendo and as 1 / T, and that the scavenging rate cancels out entirely. Each of those is a number the full model can be asked for directly, by moving one parameter and measuring the slope (Figure 5).

a result that contradicts this project's own advice

One of the four predictions holds and three do not. Confirmed: endogenous peroxide production (predicted -1, measured -0.96, -1.15). Refuted: cell density, through φ (predicted -1, measured -0.48, +0.01); read window (predicted -1, measured +0.55, +0.89); peroxide scavenging (predicted 0, measured +0.48, +0.15).

Among the failures are the two this project turned into advice. An earlier write-up recommended running at a low cell density and reading early, on the strength of the φ and T terms above. Neither term survives contact with the full model: cell density barely moves signal over background at all, and read time moves it the other way. Section 7 agrees independently - a longer read and a higher density both raise the screen's AUC. The recommendation to lower Pendo by assaying non-growing cells is untouched by this, and remains the one lever the algebra and the model agree on.

2.7The pulse, and how long to wait

The read window only decides where the integration stops, so one run gives the fold every possible window would have delivered. At the 1800 s this screen uses, the best module reaches 1.39×. It is still climbing there: the maximum is 1.45× at 3143 s, which is 1.7× the configured window (Figure 6).

3The screen

Two gates, in order, and every rejection published

3.1Two gates

Stage 1 asks whether the published cohorts separate at all - from the reported AUC where one exists, otherwise from Welch's t and Cohen's d rebuilt from the summary statistics. A biomarker that fails here is not worth a module however good the protein. Stage 2 asks whether a module could read that difference, and runs only on what survived. Rejections are listed with the stage that produced them: a screen that reports only its successes is not a screen.

3.2Stage 1: do the cohorts separate

37 published cohorts, 27 of which report an effect size this pipeline can place on a common scale. The interval matters as much as the point: several of these studies are small enough that a large effect and no effect are both inside it (Figure 7).

3.3Can the summary-statistics route be trusted

Most of clinical.csv gives a mean, a spread and two group sizes and no ROC analysis, so the screen has to rebuild the separation on the assumption that each cohort is log-normal. 7 studies report both their own AUC and enough statistics to rebuild it, which makes the assumption testable on them. The rebuilt value runs 0.040 AUC points high on average, and 0.145 at worst (Figure 8).

what this means for every AUC on this page

The screen's separations are upper bounds, not estimates. A log-normal fitted through a mean and a spread has thinner tails than the cohorts actually have, and thin tails flatter the classifier. Read every AUC below as roughly 0.04 too high, and the ranking between modules as more trustworthy than the levels.

3.4Where each module's constant falls

A module reads a patient difference best when its Michaelis or dissociation constant sits inside the clinical range. The distance between the two predicts the verdict better than anything else in the screen (Figure 9), and it is what sends most of Table 4 to the rejected pile.

4From a fold change to a diagnosis

A fold is not a test

4.1Compression, and the noise underneath it

Two things stand between a fold change and a diagnostic. The device does not report the analyte, it reports fluorescence, and the chain between them is sublinear - so the clinical difference arrives squeezed. The biological spread is squeezed by exactly the same factor, which is why the squeeze cannot be undone by rescaling afterwards. Underneath sits the reader's own error.

Everything is done on the log scale, because concentrations of urinary metabolites are roughly log-normal and because a reader CV is multiplicative. At the cut-off where the two error rates are equal, sensitivity and specificity coincide; choosing a cut-off anywhere else trades one against the other.

4.2The transfer function

Holding the healthy arm at the study's own value and sweeping the other gives the module's whole response. The two clinical levels are 13.9 and 37.3 µM; the chord between them has slope 0.331 in log-log, and that slope is the compression the propagation above uses (Figure 10).

4.3What the reader can tell apart

4.4What the device costs, and what it cannot buy

Compression is not the loss it looks like. It multiplies the clinical difference and the clinical spread by the same factor, so it cancels out of the ratio between them, and what the device actually adds is the reader's own noise alone. Across these modules that noise is 13% to 23% of the already-squeezed biological spread, and the AUC at the reader lands within 0.007 of the cohorts' own. The consequence runs both ways: a module gives up almost nothing in translation, and it can never be better than the biomarker it reads. Everything that decides this screen was decided in the clinic, before any module existed.

5Panels

One marker rarely gives usable specificity

Urinary polyamines rise in colorectal cancer - and also in benign gastrointestinal disease, which is exactly what costs the published assays their specificity. Reading several modules at once is the way out, and against a fixed skeleton that is a cloning job rather than a redesign.

How much a panel gains depends on how its markers co-vary, and that needs them measured in the same patients. Not one source in this library reports it. So each panel is given as a range over every correlation from 0 to 0.7, never as a single number, and the verdict is taken from the worst point of that range. Correlation is not simply bad, either: with unequal effect sizes the curve turns back up as ρ approaches 1, because correlated markers that respond by different amounts let their difference cancel the shared variation. That gain is real statistics and worthless here, because it exists only if ρ is known exactly.

7Sensitivity analysis

Which inputs the answer actually depends on

Every input halved and doubled in turn, with the whole chain re-run each time - fold, compression, propagation - so the bars are movements in the quantity the verdict rests on rather than in an intermediate. The baseline is AUC 0.936 for Putrescine read by PuORh.

The widest bar is OxyR cooperativity, worth 0.035 AUC points across a four-fold range, and the narrowest is membrane permeability at 0.000. The module's own measured kinetics are at the bottom of the list: Michaelis constant at 0.000 and turnover number at 0.000. That is the same conclusion the enzyme comparison reaches from the other direction - a hundred-fold spread in catalytic efficiency buys almost nothing, because the ceiling is the cell's own peroxide flux and no enzyme moves it. Scavenging is worth 0.005 here, which is the same result as section 2.5 from a different angle: it moves the resting point on the OxyR curve, and the fold between two samples depends on where that point sits.

8Honest limits

What is missing, and what was left out deliberately

No module here has been validated experimentally.

This is a designed and modelled platform, not a working one. The 6 pairs that clear both gates have cleared them on paper: against published kinetics, published cohorts and a model whose only external check is that it reproduces a peroxide gradient it was not fitted to. Nothing here has met a patient sample.

Needed and missing

  • DiAcSpm - no one has published a Michaelis constant and a turnover number (Km, kcat) for DAS oxidase (colorimetric assay), so its response cannot be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Putrescine - no one has published a dissociation constant (Kd) for PuuR, so its response cannot be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Sarcosine - no one has published a dissociation constant (Kd) for SouR (PA4184), so its response cannot be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Lactate - no one has published a dissociation constant (Kd) for LldR, so its response cannot be computed. Not estimated from a similar protein.
  • Spermidine - clinical data exists but no module in the library recognises it.

Deliberately out of scope

These are real, published, well-characterised sensors. They are excluded because they carry their own fluorophore: binding changes their fluorescence directly, with no transcription and no reporter gene, so they replace the fixed skeleton instead of plugging into it. Sarcosine (Prostate): a central value is not positive, so these are differences on a log scale already and no log-normal cohort can be rebuilt from them.[5] Sarcosine (Prostate): the study reports no central value for one of the groups.[5] Sarcosine (Prostate): the study reports no spread this pipeline can use.[6]

The platform covers small molecules recognised by an oxidase or by a metabolite-binding transcription factor; protein markers do not fit it at all. Every module shares one ceiling on sensitivity, set by the host's own peroxide flux rather than by any module. And the worked example, with every parameter exposed, is the diacetylspermine simulator.
